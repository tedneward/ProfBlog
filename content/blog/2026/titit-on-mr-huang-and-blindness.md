Jensen Huang recently was forced to step down from his role as CEO at NVidia, and less than two weeks later was brought back by the intervention of none other than the President of the United States. He was greeted by cheering employees on his return, but this story carries with it so many red flags that openly question the mindset of his defenders and apologists. The fact that so many of us are blind to see how bad this is... is telling.

<!--more-->

It seems without doubt by this point that several facts are entered into the category of "unquestionable" evidence, along with context that makes this an interesting case:

* NVidia has been a major proponent of the "AI Boom" that has driven the Mangificient Seven (Alphabet (GOOGL / GOOG), Amazon (AMZN), Apple (AAPL), Meta Platforms (META), Microsoft (MSFT), Nvidia (NVDA), and Tesla (TSLA)) stock prices sky-high and, possibly, propped up an otherwise anemic economy.
* Jensen Huang had at least four extramarital affairs, two of which were with fellow employees of NVidia who reported (either directly or indirectly) to him. [Source](https://www.forbes.com/sites/siladityaray/2026/11/24/nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-disclosed-more-affairs-to-board-before-reinstatement-report-says/)

Her firm is at the center of a pipeline that saw NVidia chips smuggled into China, and into Chinese weapons, both of which are explicitly against both US law and foreign policy. [Source](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/11/22/us/politics/alice-huang-china-spy-agency-nvidia.html)
* Jensen also engaged in several deals designed to artificially prop up the price and demand for NVidia's chips and boards, deals which seem primarily geared towards propping up the value of the stock [Source](https://fortune.com/2026/11/23/nvidia-jensen-huang-return-ai-startups-neoclouds-funding/) [Source](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/nvidias-24b-ai-deal-blitz-has-wall-street-asking-questions-about-murky-circular-investments-110039309.html). * Jensen resigned when the NVIDIA board met to determine his fate.
* Upon seeing/hearing that numerous inviduals both within and without NVidia were clamoring for his return, Jensen contacted the board about returning to the role, and was rejected.
* Trump then contacted the board members directly, and shortly thereafter the board voted to reinstate him as CEO.
* There was (and still remains!) no established successor waiting in the wings to take his place.

## The Arguments Against Jensen's Departure
The arguments criticizing Jensen's departure seem to be along the following lines:

#### It's just an affair
Hey, I get it--what goes on between a couple is nobody's business but theirs.

Except... well, let's be blunt, if the man who's in charge of one of the core companies at the heart of the AI industry has the judgement skills of a randy 13-year-old and can't keep his hands off of women other than his wife, he represents a legitimate target for the classic spycraft "honeypot" that can then leverage the scandal of an affair to either influence his corporate decisions and/or potentially gain access to materials or secrets that would otherwise remain hidden.

That Alice was able to procure chips that could then be redirected directly into China--a nation with whom the United States is supposedly in direct competition over Pacific and other global political and military theaters and markets--seems to speak clearly to the dangers of a CEO not being able to make good moral judgements.

Frankly, were this thirty years ago, the subject in question would be Bill Clinton and that intern, and the arguments made would be exactly the same. And both are correct.

This also doesn't even begin to describe the massive damage that NVidia's internal culture is about to try and reconcile. The CEO of the company engaged in not one, but FOUR violations of its company Code of Conduct, which puts the HR team in a very delicate situation--if any manager sleeps with any of their direct reports, and they terminate that manager, said manager can immediately file a wrongful termination suit citing Jensen Huang as the precedent that states that the company's policy is to put the employee on administrative leave for less than two weeks followed by reinstatement so long as that employee apologizes and begins therapy. Or, they can settle.

#### We don't know she's a spy
Yeah, we kinda do. I mean, when four different government agencies are conducting investigations, you can pretty much guarantee that the smoke leads to fire.

#### What will happen to the company/AI industry/world economy if he's gone?
This argument suggests that Huang is somehow responsible for the explosion of AI; in truth, he's just a very happy CEO whose product happened to be at the center of an evolution that was forty years in the making and reached a tipping point two years ago.

Here's what I mean: Prior to 2023, all NVidia was really responsible for doing was graphics. If you wanted to run the latest games, you needed the fat NVidia card to do all the parallel computations necessary in rendering near-real video, but beyond that, there was no practical application of NVidia chips. A few folks were experimenting with parallel computation using CUDA, but those folks were few, far between, and entirely in academic or scientific circles.

Then, ChatGPT was introduced to the world through the website, and suddenly "AI" was a thing on everybody's lips... and everybody began to realize that all of the calculations needed to run a model required the same levels of parallelization that NVidia (or any GPU, really) could provide. NVidia wasn't a prime mover-and-shaker in this scene. Universities weren't being funded by NVidia investment. It just happened.

Tell me, again, how Huang is a genius? And tell me, exactly, what we miss if he isn't in the picture?

This industry constantly deifies the CEO or CTO at the biggest corporations, when 99 times out of 100, the CEO is just the tip of a very deep--and very competent--iceberg. We thought that Steve Jobs' passing would doom Apple. It hasn't. And it hasn't really hurt Apple's innovation at all; which is to say, they're really just as un-innovative as they were before Steve's passing. Every WWDC we get new MacBookPro models with better CPUs and better RAM/storage pairings, new iPhone models (look! a foldable one that's basically the same thing Samsung has offered for several years now!), and sometimes new desktop chassis. There's been nothing new at Apple since the iPhone itself was introduced. (And yes, I do realize the iPad and iPad Mini came around, and those are basically just bigger iPhones.)

No company on the planet will be doomed to destruction by the removal of its CEO.

And as for the AI industry... well, weren't the various CEOs calling for a slowdown anyway, some time back?

#### This will tank my NVidia stock!
Oh, absolutely, and yes, it did--by half, if the Internet memes were to be believed.

If this is your argument, then you're admitting that the crime itself isn't a problem, it's the ramifications of the criminal being removed from their seat. If the fallout is too great, then the crime simply must go without correction.

Which means, then, that the individual's actions, no matter how deplorable or immoral or illegal, are all instantly forgivable if it means costing too much to the investor? Has the nation come to accept as fact the Trumpian quote from a few years ago, "I could stand in the middle of Fifth Avenue and shoot somebody, and I wouldn't lose any voters, OK?" In 2016, we were a nation that thought that statement was outrageous and clearly hyperbolic, but by 2026, we seem to be extending exactly that kind of grace to anyone who keeps our investment portfolio pointed north.

#### Those women weren't shrinking violets, anyway
This is directly echoed in "Bad guy #2" paragraph above. Apparently, a company's Code of Conduct only applies to those below a certain level of executive, and even then, if the woman in question is "confident enough" and/or compensated well enough, it's not really a problem. Power dynamics just aren't a thing there--she could've opted out whenever she wanted, after all, jobs that pay millions like that are easy to come by, even if you have a persistent rumor that you were sleeping with the boss and all the weird feelings that drags out for all parties concerned.

That there's a bunch of "tech bros" who latched on to this argument as a legitimate one is quite possibly the most obvious evidence that Silicon Valley has officially surrendered any moral authority or even baseline acknowledgement of decency. It was suspected for years that the basic perspective was "Anything goes, morals be damned" when it came to startups and investors and VCs. This just really puts the nail in the coffin.

#### If the government allowed it...
Investigations take time, and thanks to our "Innocent until proven guilty" foundation for our legal system, requires a great deal of work before any kind of movement can take place. Those investigations were still under way when the scandals broke.

Oh, and for the record, the CIA cannot operate domestically within the United States, and the FBI cannot operate internationally. The two agencies are notoriously bad at sharing data between them. And that's before we even consider the DOJ and Commerce departments being involved in this little foursome. Like it or not, to do things by the rules takes time and energy and effort, and they can't just walk up to Huang while he's having a croissant at a cafe somewhere and say, "By the way we've done some digging into your personal life and that woman you're secretly shacking up with is bad news, you should dump her."

And that's not even STARTING to imagine what Jensen's reaction would be if they actually did that. Come on, admit it, how many of you listened to even your closest friends when they told you that your most recent romantic partner was bad news?

#### No successor is not a terminable offense
Terminable, no, but not having a clear succession plan is a huge risk to any investor and the Board of Directors. For all of the reasons that the tech bros tossed around why Jensen had to come back, in fact. If a company loses its CEO in an unplanned fashion, the departure often results in the very chaos that everybody was fearing would happen to NVidia. Any executive or leader, regardless of the size of the group led, has a specific responsibility to that group to ensure that there's always a clear plan of succession in place.

The fact that Huang not only didn't have one, but *still* doesn't see having one as a necessity speaks to the relative immaturity of his experience as an executive. (Remember, he's literally only worked at one company for almost all of his professional life.)

#### Who cares if Trump intervened?
You should, actually. That the President of the United States personally inserted himself into a private corporation's private personnel matters to exert direct influence on its operations is a huge overreach of federal authority. Were this taking place in any other country on the planet, the United States would be the first to call the nation a "banana republic" and scream about "government interference". If this kind of thing is permissible, it becomes commonplace, and before long the President is running US-hosted corporations like an extension of federal policy.

The fact that Trump benefits personally from the NVidia's subsequent bump in stock price of course in no way makes this any less suspicious, either. The fact that the board rejected Huang's first return only to turn around and accept (unanimously!) his second return in no way breeds a certain air of supicion, either.

#### Who cares at ALL? It's back to profit, baby!
Except... it's not. Some of NVidia's activities have been laid out for all of their partners and competitors to see, and frankly, there's a few other chip manufacturers that could start thinking (if they haven't already been planning, building, or selling) about NVidia alternatives. Huang's judgement is clearly suspect, and if there's four affairs we know of, there's very likely more that we don't.

What's more, NVidia's board itself can no longer be trusted to be an independent body. They clearly were suborned somehow, either explicitly arm-twisted or implicitly threatened by Trump and his White House, into acting in the President's best interests. It's doubtful we will ever know the arguments that were used unless criminal charges are filed somewhere--which means it'll be a decade at least before we (the public) would get the chance to hear what those arguments were. But the fact that the board could be twisted like that is a huge red flag for anyone seriously considering doing business with NVidia beyond buying a board or laptop.

What's more, Jensen's business deals are also now out in the open, and more than a few investors may be wondering exactly how far out on the limb they're hanging. Jensen's return boosted the stock, but it's also [provoked a backlash and doesn't solve the underlying problems](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bGFLCdB7kdE). The bubble still stands at the risk of popping, and Jensen's return doesn't actually do anything to reduce that risk.

Look, I'm tired of this whole thing at this point. This is such a terrible, terrible outcome on so many levels, you almost have to look around and wonder where the cameras are that are filming this little soap opera. It's got all the hallmarks of the old "Dallas" or "Falcon Crest" of my childhood, complete with out-of-touch rich folks, sleazy businessmen, slinky women in tight cocktail dresses, and an air of "Who cares what the little people think" surrounding its principal players.

There was a time when we in this industry looked up at our leaders and said, "Gosh, those are people I'd like to be." We saw Bill Gates as the nerd-made-good, at Steve Jobs as the uncompromisingly honest visionary, and we've since come to find out that in the end, they were no better people than the robber barons we routinely panned in all the other industries: finance, oil, steel, railroad, whatever. Tech felt "clean", and now we find out that it's just as sordid at the top as any of the rest.

Remember when Google's motto was "Do no evil"? Apparently NVidia's--and all of Silicon Valley's--new corporate slogan is "Whatever turns a profit."
